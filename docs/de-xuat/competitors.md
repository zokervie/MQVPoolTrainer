# Competitive landscape: pool/billiards training apps, simulators and smart-table products (as of Nov 2026)

Note on dates: App Store pages often show a version date without the year. Where a page showed no year, these notes give the date as shown and mark it "(year not shown)". All App Store figures (prices in USD unless noted, ratings) are from the US or regional storefront pages fetched on 2026-11-11.

## Q1. Which apps/products lead, what exactly does each offer, and are they still active in 2026?

### Takeaway
The market splits into five groups: (a) camera-tracking practice apps (DrillRoom), (b) diagram/simulator "shot studio" apps (Billiard Shot Studio, WPB, PoolCuelator, Bricole, AimPool), (c) stroke/cue-ball sensors (DigiCue Blue, DigiBall), (d) projector + vision table systems (IQ Reality, Eddi, Magixpool, FusionCue beta), and (e) casual/semi-sim 3D games (Virtual Pool, Pro Pool 2026, Billiards City). No single iOS product combines a credible physics simulator, structured drills with skill rating, and online play. That combination is the gap ZokerPoolTrainer can target.

### Cited Findings

**Camera / AI practice tracking**
- DrillRoom: AI Pool Trainer (OrangeLoops) is still live: v3.1.1 (Jul 24, year not shown), requires iOS 18, rated 4.6 from 258 ratings. It uses a camera on a tripod about 5 ft high to auto-track shots and gives real-time audio coaching. It reports shotmaking % and speed stats, pocketing heatmaps and progress charts. It has a drill editor that can capture real layouts by live detection, an Apple Watch companion, and drill sharing and tagging (v3.0). Subscriber-only features: post-drill analysis diagrams, shot video clips, multiple profiles, and 50+ drills, including "Billiard University Exam I", Golf Pool and Centerfield Infinite — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1539827505)
- DrillRoom has been marketed as "the first AI-powered billiard training app" since about 2021 — [The Pool Scene](https://sub.thepoolscene.com/posts/drillroom-the-first-ai-powered-billiard-training-app-launches-a-major-update); [OrangeLoops](https://orangeloops.com/2021/02/drillroom-ai-powered-billiard-training-ios-app/)

**Shot simulators / diagram apps**
- Billiard Shot Studio (Razor Pool, LLC): v3.1.0 (Oct 20, year not shown), rated 4.7 from 36 ratings. It simulates pool 7/8/9 ft, Heyball, snooker 10/12 ft and carom. Users set aim, speed, spin, cue elevation and cue-ball deflection (squirt). Playback goes down to 1/32× slow motion and supports frame stepping, with a spin indicator and motion trails. Planning tools include "Find Positioning Shot", "Shape Zones" and "Pocket Aim Lock". Shots can be shared by link or QR code and exported as images or video. The free tier is limited to 2 object balls — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/billiard-shot-studio/id6504303931)
- WPB: Pool Training & Drills (Parrent Ventures): v4.4.6 (Oct 4, year not shown), rated 4.4 from 252 ratings. It has 200+ scored drills with progress tracking and **weekly leaderboards**, a ghost-ball / contact-point aiming calculator, and a break-speed calculator (Premium). It also offers a structured course, a table layout creator for sharing drills, a tournament manager, a shot clock and community features — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1591274006)
- PoolCuelator (indie): free, no ads or IAP, offline. Covers American pool, blackball and snooker, with drag-and-drop balls and a free-draw tool for shot paths. No ratings yet — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/poolcuelator/id6740300570)
- AimPool: Billiards Shot Coach (Aziz Saba, 2026, v0.90): the user photographs a real table, confirms the 4 corners, and the app detects the balls on-device and recommends a shot. A 3D view shows the aim line, contact point, ball paths and cue-ball position, with advice on pots, banks, kicks, safeties, power and spin. Also covers snooker, English billiards and carom, with lessons, CPU/2P matches and Apple Watch scoring. No ratings yet — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6789332083)
- Bricole (Kayra Tozan; v1.0 Jul 2, v1.3.5 Sep 15, years not shown): a three-cushion carom solver covering 8 diamond systems, with kiss warnings. It offers camera-assisted table calibration, 64 drills with mastery stars, weekly focus topics, a Daily Position puzzle with streaks, practice plans, a Shot Notebook and offline use. Notably it has a **Vietnamese UI** — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/bricole/id6782375615)
- Other 2025–26 aiming apps exist (Billiards Aiming Assistant, Pool Shot Calculator & Trainer, Cue Lab, "Aim Master 8 Ball Pool", which is aimed at the 8 Ball Pool game, not real tables). Their listings were not fetched — [search results: Billiards Aiming Assistant](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1626264164), [Pool Shot Calculator & Trainer](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6775273502), [Cue Lab](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1480774300), [Pool & Billiards Self-Trainer](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6748546651)

**Stroke / cue-ball sensors**
- DigiCue Blue (Nathan Rhoades LLC): a cue-mounted sensor that vibrates when it detects an inconsistent stroke (e.g., follow-through). The new "DigiCue App" is v3.0.3 (14 Oct 2025) and free. It has customizable thresholds, shot history, session grouping and threshold lines on graphs — [App Store DE](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/digicue-app/id6476167920)
- The old DigiCue Blue app was pulled from the App Store. A July 2025 user reports the new app occasionally fails to log shots and lacks a "time since last shot" indicator, but is otherwise very positive — [AZB forum](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/update-on-digicue-blue-7-21-25.577942/). A firmware update thread exists — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/posts/8440197/)
- DigiBall (same maker): a regulation-weight, phenolic electronic cue ball with an IMU. It sends tip contact point (as % of radius), English angle, spin in RPS and speed over Bluetooth. Other features: shot clock with voice, scatter plots with mean/SD, spin-over-time plots, jump and collision detection, exportable timestamped data for video sync, and Apple Watch support. It cannot measure stroke straightness, cue elevation or object-ball contact. The page still says "Coming Soon", no price — [DigiBall page](https://sites.google.com/view/nrllc/pages/digiball)

**Projector + vision systems (table hardware)**
- IQ Reality "IQ AR Projection System": a ceiling-mounted AR projector controlled from an iOS/Android app or by cue/hand gesture. It has 22 themes, 7 games ("virtual line assistant", 8/9-ball tracking, Speed Pool, etc.) and 6 training modes with "hundreds of modules", including an "IQ Test". It is Windows-based and needs a ceiling of at least 8.75 ft. Sale price was $10,000 (sold out) — [Touch of Modern](https://www.touchofmodern.com/sales/iq-reality/iq-ar-projection-system-black). A YouTube comment cited on AZB puts it at about $7,500 — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/new-a-projection-pool-system-with-built-in-vision.576925/)
- Eddi Virtual Pool Coach (eddiwins.com): a projector, camera and ring light mounted over the table. It auto-scores (e.g., target pool) and illustrates the physics. It was in beta in June 2025 and the thread was still active in March 2026. Price unconfirmed (about $3K per forum speculation) — [AZB thread](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/new-a-projection-pool-system-with-built-in-vision.576925/)
- Magixpool (TouchMagix): projection pool installed at Dave & Buster's locations, about $15K per AZB — [Arcade Heroes 2023](https://arcadeheroes.com/2023/06/26/touch-magix-to-launch-magixpool-at-bowl-expo-2023/); [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/new-a-projection-pool-system-with-built-in-vision.576925/)
- FusionCue (indie, pre-release in 2026): an overhead webcam with a **local AI vision model** ("no special balls, no sensors, no cloud") plus a projector. It does live tracking and auto-scoring, and supports **online multiplayer in which the opponent's table is projected onto yours**. There is a 60-member Discord beta, no price — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/fusioncue-the-most-advanced-pool-training-system-is-almost-here-video-demo.588792/post-8481386)
- Plain projector systems without vision sell for about $1,500 — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/new-a-projection-pool-system-with-built-in-vision.576925/)
- Smart tables: Diamond markets a "Diamond Smart Pool Table", but its product page lists only hardware (ball return, slate, cushions) and gives no electronics or price — [Diamond](https://diamondbilliards.com/products/diamond-smart-pool-table). A 2026 retailer article on "smart tables" names no concrete models or prices (only Poolhouse/ForeVR in VR) — [Quarter King](https://quarterkingbilliards.com/smart-pool-tables-2026-ai-led-lighting-and-the-future-of-billiards-at-home/)

**3D simulators / games on iOS**
- Virtual Pool (Celeris; mobile version of Virtual Pool 4): v4.33 (Oct 22, year not shown), rated 3.8 from 101 ratings. It has Pro Tour and Hustler careers, 128 AI opponents and 26 extra games in packs. Online play via Game Center uses paid "Online Credits". It has a Cue Ball Tracking feature and break/jump/low-deflection cues. A reviewer praises its squirt, cut-induced throw and rail physics — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/virtual-pool/id1183149712); Steam [VP4](https://store.steampowered.com/app/336150)
- Pro Pool 2026 (iWare Designs): v1.45 (Oct 6, year not shown), rated 4.6 from about 1.7K ratings. It claims "full 3D rigid body physics at 60 FPS". It has free online and local-network multiplayer, 8 game variants (incl. 14.1 and Chinese 8-ball), 7/8/9 ft and novelty tables, 25 CPU opponents over 5 levels, Rookie-to-Legend ranks and Game Center achievements — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id556429076)
- Open-source web sim tailuge/billiards (blog April 2026) implements the Mathavan et al. (2010) cushion model with spin-dependent rebound and throw. It argues most games use mirror reflection and ignore spin and throw — [blog](https://billiards.tailuge.workers.dev/blog1.html)

**Ratings / standardized tests**
- Dr. Dave's Billiard University (BU) Playing-Ability Exam is a standardized skill test with published score-to-rating tables. It is already embedded as a drill in DrillRoom — [AZB BU exam thread](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/billiard-university-bu-playing-ability-exam-scores-and-ratings.321301/post-6179590); [DrillRoom](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1539827505)
- Dr. Dave's FAQ explains how FargoRate works — [drdavepoolinfo FAQ](https://drdavepoolinfo.com/faq/rating/fargorate/)

### Inferences
- The camera-tracking practice niche is effectively DrillRoom alone on iOS, and it is expensive. Projector-plus-vision setups cost $1.5K–$15K and need ceiling installation. A phone-only app that predicts shots from a single photo (as AimPool does) is the low-cost alternative now appearing.
- Simulator apps with real physics knobs (Billiard Shot Studio: squirt, elevation, 1/32 slow-motion) set the bar for an "analysis mode". ZokerPoolTrainer's C++ physics with throw, squirt and swerve plus deterministic replay is directly comparable and should beat this on fidelity and validation (e.g., published Mathavan cushion behaviour).
- Pro Pool 2026 already offers free online and LAN play, so Wi-Fi play alone is not a differentiator. Training-oriented online play is (FusionCue's remote-drill challenge idea, WPB's weekly leaderboards).
- Bricole has a Vietnamese UI and a three-cushion focus, so it is a direct competitor for Vietnamese carom players.

## Q2. Which features are unique and loved?

### Takeaway
Users love automatic scoring with no manual logging (DrillRoom, sensors), objective metrics with statistics (DigiBall scatter and SD, DrillRoom heatmaps), slow-motion physics playback for understanding misses (Shot Studio), shareable drills and layouts, and leaderboards or exams that give a "rating".

### Cited Findings
- DrillRoom reviewers praise automatic scoring and the shot analysis plus video for finding weaknesses, and call it "the best AR pool training tool". Complaints: it sometimes misses ball-ball contact, ends drills unexpectedly, setup is finicky, and users want TV mirroring — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1539827505)
- Billiard Shot Studio reviewers praise intuitive controls and detailed physics. Teachers use the animated cue-ball and object-ball paths, and players use it to test why a miss happened — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/billiard-shot-studio/id6504303931)
- WPB reviewers like self-paced drills and the tournament bracket and scoring tools — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1591274006)
- DigiCue user: consistent "Follow Through 10" scores after the change, i.e., vibration feedback drives habit change — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/update-on-digicue-blue-7-21-25.577942/)
- Pro Pool reviewers say it "helped them improve their real-table play" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id556429076)
- Projection/vision: users want tracked practice, shot streaks and replays, and shared pool-hall use with phones as controllers — [AZB Eddi thread](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/new-a-projection-pool-system-with-built-in-vision.576925/). FusionCue's remote multiplayer drew "would buy right away, even in beta" — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/fusioncue-the-most-advanced-pool-training-system-is-almost-here-video-demo.588792/post-8481386)
- Gamified retention tools in 2026 apps: Daily Position puzzle with streaks, mastery stars, weekly focus (Bricole) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/bricole/id6782375615); weekly leaderboards (WPB) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1591274006)
- Apple Watch companions are common (DrillRoom, DigiBall, AimPool) — [DrillRoom](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1539827505), [DigiBall](https://sites.google.com/view/nrllc/pages/digiball), [AimPool](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6789332083)

### Inferences
- Features to adopt: a standardized exam (a BU-style or own "Zoker Exam") giving a rating; a daily puzzle with streaks; drills shareable by link or QR code; slow-motion replay with spin indicator and trails; scatter or SD statistics for aim error.
- Features to beat: tracking reliability (DrillRoom's main complaint). In a pure-simulation app, deterministic physics avoids missed detection entirely. In the analysis mode, show *why* a shot missed (throw or squirt contribution in degrees), which no competitor quantifies.
- Pure aim training without aids fits ZokerPoolTrainer's rule of "no aiming help in main mode". Competitors mostly add aim aids (ghost ball, aim lines), so "no-aid eye training plus post-shot ideal-line reveal" is a differentiator.

### Gaps
- No large-sample review corpus for DrillRoom/WPB (only 4 visible reviews each). Reddit r/billiards threads could not be retrieved directly in this pass.

## Q3. What do users commonly complain about?

### Takeaway
The top complaints are intrusive ads and pay-to-play friction, physics or aim inconsistency ("not accurate", skewed bounces, unrealistic English), AI difficulty that is badly calibrated, unreliable tracking or sensor logging, and hardware cost and installation.

### Cited Findings
- Billiards City (Pooking): 41,291 reviews analysed, 33.4% negative. Ads are the most common complaint (after every round, some ads can't be exited). Physics and aiming complaints include "not accurate at all", straight shots missing, unrealistic English curves, aim drifting during a shot and out-of-proportion pockets and angles. Players also report accidental shots from the slider and want to disable "the hand" — [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1299421779/pooking-billiards-city/reviews)
- Pro Pool 2026: ads after every rack, chalk only via ads, lowest AI too strong, League/Tournament behind paywall — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id556429076)
- Virtual Pool: AI too weak (snooker), a control-scheme change upset users, repeated prompts to buy money — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/virtual-pool/id1183149712)
- WPB: the free tier is "unusable without paying", and an iPad Pro landscape bug hid the login button — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1591274006)
- DrillRoom: missed contacts, drills ending unexpectedly, finicky setup — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1539827505)
- DigiCue: dropped shots, stalled app hard to detect — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/update-on-digicue-blue-7-21-25.577942/)
- Projector systems: "$3,000 in lessons would do more", gimmicky, unclear pricing, beta status — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/new-a-projection-pool-system-with-built-in-vision.576925/). Calibration and assembly are hard for typical players, box lights block cameras, 8 ft ceilings are too low — [AZB FusionCue](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/fusioncue-the-most-advanced-pool-training-system-is-almost-here-video-demo.588792/post-8481386)
- Physics critique: most games use mirror cushion reflection and ignore throw and spin transfer, so they "look like pool but don't play like it" — [tailuge blog, Apr 2026](https://billiards.tailuge.workers.dev/blog1.html)

### Inferences
- ZokerPoolTrainer should ship with no forced ads. Also: fine aim control (nudge or vernier, no drift, a guard against accidental shots), CPU difficulty tiers validated against human data, and an iPad landscape QA pass.
- Publishing physics-validation evidence (test cases against Mathavan/Alciatore references) is a marketing asset against the "physics feels fake" complaint.

## Q4. Pricing and monetization models

### Takeaway
Trainers use freemium subscriptions ($3.99–$14.99/month, $20–$90/year) with optional lifetime unlocks ($18–$250). Games use ad-supported play plus small modular IAPs ($0.99–$9.99) or game packs. Hardware ranges from sensors (price unknown) to projector systems ($1.5K–$15K).

### Cited Findings
- DrillRoom: $14.99/month, $89.99/year — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1539827505)
- WPB: $10.99/month, $79.99/year, $249.99 lifetime, 7-day trial — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1591274006)
- Billiard Shot Studio: $3.99/month, $29.99/year, $79.99 lifetime — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/billiard-shot-studio/id6504303931)
- AimPool: $19.99/year (7-day trial), $29.99 premium — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6789332083)
- Bricole Pro: CHF 4/month, CHF 35/year, CHF 18 lifetime (as listed) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/bricole/id6782375615)
- PoolCuelator: free, no ads or IAP — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/poolcuelator/id6740300570)
- Pro Pool 2026: modular IAP. Rules packs $0.99, Tournament and League modes $3.99 each, novelty tables $0.99, Pro Chalk $3.99, No Ads $7.99, complete game $9.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id556429076)
- Virtual Pool: game packs $1.99–$4.99, career cash $0.99–$2.99, 25 online credits $0.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/virtual-pool/id1183149712)
- DigiCue App: free (the hardware is sold separately) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/digicue-app/id6476167920)
- Hardware: IQ AR projector $10,000 sale price (sold out) — [Touch of Modern](https://www.touchofmodern.com/sales/iq-reality/iq-ar-projection-system-black). Magixpool about $15K, Eddi about $3K (unconfirmed), vision-less projectors about $1.5K — [AZB](https://forums.azbilliards.com/threads/new-a-projection-pool-system-with-built-in-vision.576925/)

### Inferences
- ZokerPoolTrainer's planned IAP packs fit the Pro Pool model (modular one-time unlocks), which avoids subscription fatigue. Users explicitly asked for one-time options: one Billiards City reviewer offered $2.99 to remove ads and $4.99 for 8/9-ball solo — [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1299421779/pooking-billiards-city/reviews)
- A hybrid of one-time drill/curriculum packs ($2.99–$9.99) plus an optional all-access lifetime unlock in the $30–$80 range would undercut DrillRoom and WPB subscriptions. A generous free tier avoids WPB's "unusable without paying" reviews.
